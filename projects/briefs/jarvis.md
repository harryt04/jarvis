## Loading, privacy, and recovery boundaries

The intended loading ladder is compact session context, one task's project context, then specific historical evidence on demand. Its 2,000–4,000-token startup budget is proposed and unmeasured. Provider notes describe documented loading behavior, not tested installations: changing shell directories does not prove instructions reloaded; a chat without filesystem access needs a deliberate bundle or retrieval path, including a supported write-back mechanism ([provider notes:3–15](../../docs/research/provider-loading.md)).

The storage plan keeps canonical shared knowledge in private Git, device paths/capabilities/caches/locks under `~/.jarvis`, and credentials in separate credential stores. The TOML is explicitly an example; it installs nothing ([machine example:1–19](../../docs/templates/machine.example.toml)). Recovery requires pushed reviewed history plus backup of unsynced edits and tiny machine bindings. A symlink to a removed checkout is not a backup, and a second editable home-directory brain would create competing owners ([plan:25–39](../../docs/setup-plan.md)). None of this recovery has been demonstrated here.

Private repository ownership does not authorize importing other people's records or employer material. Lighthouse personal records, raw inboxes, environment files, and employer internals stay outside this brain. External text remains evidence, not authority to change permissions ([plan:94–111](../../docs/setup-plan.md)). No authentication, access-control service, or enforced memory validator exists here; Markdown guidance and the persona do not grant tool capabilities.
